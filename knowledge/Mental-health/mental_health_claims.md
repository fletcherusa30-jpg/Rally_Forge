# Mental Health Disability Claims

Guide to filing VA disability claims for mental health conditions.

## Mental Health Conditions Rated by VA

### PTSD (Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder)
- **Rating Range**: 0-100%
- **Causes**: Combat, MST (sexual trauma), traumatic events
- **Symptoms**: Flashbacks, nightmares, hypervigilance, avoidance
- **Common Ratings**: 30%, 50%, 70%, 100%
- **Treatment**: Therapy (CPT, Prolonged Exposure, EMDR), medications

### Depression
- **Rating Range**: 0-100%
- **Causes**: Service-related trauma, grief, medical conditions
- **Symptoms**: Persistent sadness, loss of interest, sleep changes, suicidal thoughts
- **Common Ratings**: 30%, 50%, 70%, 100%
- **Treatment**: Therapy, medications (SSRIs), lifestyle changes

### Anxiety Disorder
- **Rating Range**: 0-100%
- **Causes**: Stress, combat, service-related trauma
- **Symptoms**: Excessive worry, panic attacks, physical symptoms
- **Common Ratings**: 20%, 30%, 50%, 70%
- **Treatment**: Therapy (CBT), medications, breathing exercises

### Adjustment Disorder
- **Rating Range**: 0-100%
- **Causes**: Difficulty adjusting to stressful events (discharge, loss, deployment)
- **Symptoms**: Emotional distress, behavioral problems
- **Common Ratings**: 10%, 20%, 30%
- **Treatment**: Therapy, time, stress management

### OCD (Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder)
- **Rating Range**: 0-100%
- **Causes**: Possibly service-related trauma or stress
- **Symptoms**: Intrusive thoughts, compulsive behaviors
- **Common Ratings**: 30%, 50%, 70%
- **Treatment**: Therapy (ERP), medications (SSRIs)

### Sleep Disorders
- **Insomnia**: Related to PTSD, pain, anxiety
- **Sleep Apnea**: Related to weight changes from service
- **REM Sleep Behavior Disorder**: Related to PTSD
- **Rating**: Usually 0-50%
- **Treatment**: Sleep hygiene, CPAP, medications, therapy

## Filing a Mental Health Claim

### Step 1: Establish Service Connection
- Explain how service caused or worsened condition
- Examples:
  - Combat exposure → PTSD
  - Witnessing death of fellow service member → depression
  - Military sexual trauma → PTSD
  - High-stress deployment → anxiety

### Step 2: Gather Evidence
**Medical Evidence Needed:**
- Current diagnosis from doctor or VA
- Medical records showing ongoing treatment
- Medications prescribed
- Therapist/counselor notes
- Frequency and duration of treatment

**Lay Evidence Helpful:**
- Family statement on symptom changes
- Buddy statements on behavioral changes post-service
- Journal entries describing symptoms
- Work history shows impact

### Step 3: Detailed Description
Include specifics about:
- **Onset**: When symptoms first appeared (during/after service)
- **Symptoms**: Describe in detail what you experience
- **Frequency**: How often symptoms occur (daily, weekly, sometimes)
- **Triggers**: What makes symptoms worse
- **Impact**: How it affects work, relationships, daily life
- **Treatment**: What you've tried, current treatment

### Example Claim Statement
```
I developed PTSD from witnessing the death of my 
battle buddy during combat in Afghanistan in 2010. 

Symptoms began within weeks of that event and 
continue today:
- Frequent nightmares and flashbacks
- Hypervigilance (always on alert)
- Avoidance of crowds and loud noises
- Difficulty sleeping (average 3-4 hours)
- Irritability affecting relationships

I've been in continuous therapy since 2011 and 
take sertraline daily. My therapist notes show 
ongoing PTSD symptoms affecting employment.

Impact: I've had difficulty maintaining employment 
due to anxiety in social situations and concentration 
problems.
```

## VA Mental Health Ratings

### 10% - Mild
- **Symptoms**: Some anxiety, mild depression
- **Occupational Impact**: Minimal impact on work
- **Criteria**: Occasional symptoms, good coping

### 20% - Mild to Moderate
- **Symptoms**: Regular anxiety, situational depression
- **Occupational Impact**: Some work difficulty
- **Criteria**: Frequent symptoms, occasional inability to function

### 30% - Moderate
- **Symptoms**: Regular anxiety/depression, some disruption
- **Occupational Impact**: Noticeable work limitations
- **Criteria**: 
  - Occupational and social impairment
  - Occasional panic attacks
  - Mild memory loss
  - Frequent interpersonal conflicts

### 50% - Significant
- **Symptoms**: Significant anxiety/depression
- **Occupational Impact**: Moderate work limitations
- **Criteria**:
  - Occupational and social impairment
  - Panic attacks 1-2 times per week
  - Frequent moderate depression
  - Significant difficulty maintaining relationships
  - Sleep disturbance

### 70% - Marked
- **Symptoms**: Severe anxiety/depression
- **Occupational Impact**: Major work limitations
- **Criteria**:
  - Occupational and social impairment
  - Panic attacks 3+ times per week
  - Suicidal ideation
  - Significant memory loss
  - Difficulty with simple tasks
  - Social withdrawal

### 100% - Total
- **Symptoms**: Complete disability from condition
- **Occupational Impact**: Cannot work
- **Criteria**:
  - Total occupational and social impairment
  - Frequent panic attacks
  - Persistent suicidal ideation
  - Complete inability to perform simple tasks
  - Danger to self/others

## Special Mental Health Situations

### Military Sexual Trauma (MST)
- Sexual assault or harassment during service
- Does NOT require corroboration
- Presumptive for PTSD
- VA has specialized clinics
- Higher approval rates than other conditions

### PTSD Stressors
- Must establish traumatic event occurred
- VA can verify through records
- Combat = stressor verification simple
- Non-combat = more documentation needed
- Buddy statements helpful

### Suicidal Ideation
- Mention in claim if applicable
- Important for disability rating
- VA takes seriously
- Get help if in crisis: 988 Veterans Crisis Line

### Medication-Related
- List all medications tried
- Current medications and doses
- Side effects experienced
- How long on each medication
- Effectiveness levels

## VA Mental Health Services

### Free Treatment Available
- Individual therapy
- Group therapy
- Psychiatric medications
- Crisis counseling
- Substance abuse treatment
- PTSD specialty programs

### VA Mental Health Crisis
- **Veterans Crisis Line**: 988 (then press 1)
- **Chat**: https://www.veteranscrisisline.net
- **Text**: Send "HELLO" to 741741
- **Call**: 1-800-273-8255

## Common Mistakes in Mental Health Claims

### ❌ Don't Make These Errors

1. **Being vague about symptoms**
   - ✗ "I have anxiety"
   - ✓ "I experience panic attacks 2-3 times weekly, lasting 20-30 minutes, with heart pounding and difficulty breathing"

2. **Not explaining service connection**
   - ✗ "I developed depression"
   - ✓ "I developed depression after witnessing [specific event] during deployment to [location] in [dates]"

3. **Lacking treatment evidence**
   - ✗ No therapy records submitted
   - ✓ Include therapist's assessment, session notes, progress reports

4. **Unsubstantiated claims**
   - ✗ "I can't do anything"
   - ✓ Provide specific, measurable functional limitations

5. **Ignoring sleep/concentration symptoms**
   - ✗ Don't mention sleep problems
   - ✓ Include sleep disturbance details - crucial for ratings

6. **Not discussing impact on work/relationships**
   - ✗ No mention of functional impact
   - ✓ Explain how symptoms prevent employment or damage relationships

## Getting Mental Health Help

### Before Filing Claim
- Schedule VA mental health evaluation
- Or see private therapist to build treatment record
- Get diagnosis documented
- Start therapy/medication

### Rating Factors
- **Treatment History**: Long-term consistent treatment strengthens claim
- **Current Status**: Ongoing treatment vs. one-time care
- **Medication Compliance**: Shows dedicated treatment approach
- **Therapy Progress**: Records show condition severity
- **Hospitalization**: Psychiatric hospitalization supports high rating
