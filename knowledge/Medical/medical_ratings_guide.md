# Medical Evidence and Disability Ratings

How VA evaluates medical conditions for disability compensation.

## Disability Evaluation Process

### Step 1: Establish Service Connection
- Condition must be related to military service
- From: During service, immediately after, or related to service
- Evidence: Medical records, service records, nexus letter

### Step 2: VA Schedules C&P Exam
- Compensation & Pension examination
- Independent medical professional evaluates
- Based on VA rating schedule
- Results guide rating decision

### Step 3: VA Rates Condition
- Percentage: 0%, 10%, 20%, ... 100%
- Based on severity and functional impact
- Using VA Schedule for Rating Disabilities
- Effective date determined

### Step 4: Combined Rating Calculated
- Multiple conditions combined
- Using VA's Combined Ratings Table
- Example: 50% + 30% + 20% = 73%
- Higher rating = higher monthly pay

## Disability Ratings Explained

### 0% - Service Connection Granted
- Condition service-connected but not disabling
- No monthly payment
- Access to VA healthcare
- Can increase later if worsens

### 10% - Minimal Disability
- Mild symptoms present
- Minimal impact on daily life
- Examples: Slight hearing loss, mild arthritis
- **2026 Rate**: ~$172/month (veteran alone)

### 20% - Mild Disability
- Noticeable symptoms
- Some impact on daily activities
- Examples: Controlled anxiety, 20-25% hearing loss
- **2026 Rate**: ~$343/month

### 30% - Moderate Disability
- Regular symptoms affecting daily function
- Some limitations on work/activity
- Examples: Moderate PTSD, 40-50% hearing loss
- **2026 Rate**: $537/month (veteran alone)
- **With 1 Dependent**: +$49/month
- **Each Additional Child**: +$63/month

### 40% - Significant Disability
- Substantial impact on daily activities
- Notable work restrictions
- Examples: Severe PTSD with medication, chronic back pain
- **2026 Rate**: $730/month (veteran alone)
- **With Spouse**: +$83/month
- **Each Child**: +$76/month

### 50% - Major Disability
- Significant functional impairment
- Can work but with limitations
- Examples: Moderate brain injury, diabetes with complications
- **2026 Rate**: $1,075/month (veteran alone)
- **With Spouse**: +$104/month (Total: $1,179/month)
- **Per Child**: +$62/month each (up to 3 typically)

### 60% - Severe Disability
- Major life limitations
- May be difficult to work
- Examples: Moderate TBI, severe PTSD, moderate MS
- **2026 Rate**: $1,362/month (veteran alone)
- **With Spouse**: +$125/month
- **Each Child**: +$77/month

### 70% - Very Severe Disability
- Substantial limitations
- Work dependent on condition stability
- Examples: Service-connected blindness in one eye
- **2026 Rate**: $1,716/month (veteran alone)
- **With Spouse**: +$145/month
- **Each Child**: +$90/month

### 80% - Critical Disability
- Near-total functional impairment
- Very limited work capability
- Examples: Severe TBI, service-connected blindness both eyes
- **2026 Rate**: $1,995/month (veteran alone)
- **With Spouse**: +$166/month
- **Each Child**: +$103/month

### 90% - Severe Disability
- Extreme limitations
- May prevent substantial gainful employment
- Examples: Total deafness, severe combined conditions
- **2026 Rate**: $2,242/month (veteran alone)
- **With Spouse**: +$187/month
- **Each Child**: +$116/month

### 100% - Total Disability
- Completely prevent gainful employment
- Monthly payment: Full civilian salary equivalent
- **2026 Rate**: $3,738/month (veteran alone)
- **With Spouse**: +$208/month (Total: $3,946/month)
- **Per Child**: +$144/month each
- **With 1 Spouse + 3 Children**: $4,170/month
- Special consideration for TDIU (Totally Disabled Individual Unemployable)

## VA Rating Schedule

VA uses a standardized schedule for each condition type:

### Musculoskeletal (Joint Disabilities)
- Range: 0-70%
- Based on: Range of motion, functional limitation
- Examples:
  - Knee: 20% (slight limitation) to 60% (severe)
  - Shoulder: 10% (mild) to 50% (severe)

### Mental Health (psychiatric conditions)
- Range: 0-100%
- Based on: Severity of symptoms, occupational/social impairment
- Examples:
  - PTSD: 10-100% depending on symptoms
  - Depression: 10-100%
  - Anxiety: 0-100%

### Cardiovascular
- Range: 0-100%
- Based on: Functional limitations, test results
- Examples:
  - Hypertension: 0-20%
  - Coronary artery disease: 20-100%

### Respiratory
- Range: 0-100%
- Based on: Breathing capacity, oxygen requirements
- Examples:
  - Asthma: 10-80%
  - Sleep apnea: 0-50%

### Neurological (Brain/Nerve)
- Range: 0-100%
- Based on: Severity and impact on function
- Examples:
  - Traumatic brain injury: 10-100%
  - Migraine headaches: 0-50%

### Visual
- Range: 0-100%
- Based on: Visual acuity in better eye
- Examples:
  - 20/100 vision: 30%
  - Total blindness: 100%

### Hearing Loss
- Rating: 0-100%
- Based on: Speech discrimination ability
- Usually combined with tinnitus

## Special Rating Situations

### SMC - Special Monthly Compensation
- Additional payment for severe disabilities
- Levels K through T
- Examples:
  - Loss of both hands: SMC-L
  - Aid and Attendance need: SMC-L/M/N
  - Housebound: SMC-S/T
  - Combination of severe conditions

### TDIU - Total Disability Individual Unemployable
- 50 or 60% rating qualifying for 100% pay
- Must: Unable to work due to service-connected conditions
- Can receive 100% disability compensation without 100% rating

### P&T - Permanent & Total
- 100% rating that won't be reviewed
- Stable condition (won't improve)
- Lifetime benefits unchanged

### IU - Individual Unemployable
- Same as TDIU
- Requires rating from VA VARO
- Must prove condition prevents gainful employment

## C&P Exam Tips

### Prepare For Exam
- Bring all relevant medical records
- List of current medications
- Write down symptoms you experience
- Bring photos of functional limitations

### During Exam
- Be honest about only what you experience
- Describe your worst-case scenario
- Don't exaggerate or minimize
- Ask clarifying questions
- Take notes on what was examined

### After Exam
- Request a copy of exam results
- Review for accuracy
- Dispute errors before rating decision
- Follow up if rating seems low

## Increasing Your Rating

### File Claim for Increase
- Current condition has worsened
- Submit VA Form 21-0996 or claim online
- Provide new medical evidence
- Can cite recent exams/hospitalizations

### Grounds for Increase
- Condition definitely worse than latest rating
- New medical records support higher rating
- Progressive condition (diabetes, arthritis)
- New symptoms identified

### Timeline
- VA will schedule new C&P exam if needed
- Decision usually within 3-6 months
- Increase effective date determined
- Back pay provided if approved
