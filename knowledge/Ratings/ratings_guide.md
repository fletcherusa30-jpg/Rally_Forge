# Understanding Disability Ratings

**Know Your Rating & How Much Money You Should Get**

A disability rating is the **percentage (0%–100%)** that VA assigns to show how your condition affects your ability to work and live. 

**Your rating determines your monthly payment.** A higher rating = more money.

This guide explains how ratings work, what affects them, and how to make sure you get the rating you deserve.

## Quick Overview: What Your Rating Means (And What You Get Paid)

**Common Misconception**: "I'm 20% disabled"  
**What it Actually Means**: "My condition causes enough limitation that VA rates it at 20%, so I get $343/month"

| Your Rating | Monthly Payment (Veteran Alone) | What This Means |
|---|---|---|
| **0%** | $0 | Service-connected, no current disability |
| **10%** | $172 | Mild symptoms, mostly able to work |
| **20%** | $343 | Regular symptoms, can work with difficulty |
| **30%** | $537 | Moderate symptoms, noticeable work impact |
| **40%** | $730 | Significant work limitations |
| **50%** | $1,075 | Major work difficulties, but can work |
| **60%** | $1,362 | Substantial work limitations |
| **70%** | $1,716 | Severe limitations, hard to work |
| **80%** | $1,995 | Very severe limitations |
| **90%** | $2,242 | Nearly total disability |
| **100% (Permanent & Total)** | $3,738 | Cannot work; deemed permanently disabled |

*Rates effective December 1, 2025 (includes 2.5% COLA increase)*

## How VA Rates Conditions {#how-va-rates}

### The Rating Process: Step-by-Step

**Step 1: Service Connection Established**
- Your condition is determined to be service-related
- This happens before rating (see [Service Connection Guide](../Service-connection/service_connection_guide.md))
- If not service-connected, you get no rating

**Step 2: You're Scheduled for C&P Exam**
- C&P = Compensation & Pension medical evaluation
- VA-contracted doctor examines you (or VA doctor)
- Usually 30–60 minutes
- Doctor documents your symptoms, limitations, and functional abilities

**Step 3: Rater Reviews All Evidence**
- VA rating specialist reviews: C&P exam results, medical records, evidence you submitted
- Applies VA Disability Rating Schedule (38 CFR Part 4)
- Determines which rating percentage best fits your condition

**Step 4: Rating Decision Issued**
- You receive letter explaining the rating
- Shows the effective date (when benefits began)
- Lists the diagnosis and rating percentage
- Explains your appeal rights

---

### What the VA Rater Looks For

VA raters use a **disability schedule** for each condition type. They consider:

**1. Severity of Symptoms**
- Are symptoms mild, moderate, or severe?
- How much do they affect your daily life?
- Are they stable, improving, or getting worse?

**2. Functional Limitations**
- What can't you do because of this condition?
- Can you stand, sit, lift, walk without problems?
- Can you remember, concentrate, socialize?
- Can you work? For how long each day?

**3. Treatment & Medication**
- What medications do you take?
- How often do you need medical care?
- Are there side effects affecting function?
- Do you need surgery or ongoing therapy?

**4. Evidence Quality**
- Recent medical records (stronger)
- Doctor's specific functional assessment (stronger)
- Your detailed description of limitations (helpful)
- Vague complaints without medical backing (weaker)

---

### Key Rule: "Essentially Compatible"

VA must give you the rating that is "essentially compatible" with your symptoms and limitations as shown in medical evidence.

**This means:**
- Rating should match your actual condition (not too low, not inflated)
- If evidence shows 30% symptoms, you shouldn't get 10%
- If evidence supports 50%, VA shouldn't artificially lower to 40%
- If evidence is unclear, VA gives you the benefit of the doubt

### Dependent Additions (How Much Each Dependent Adds)

When you have a spouse and/or children, each dependent adds the following to your base rating:

**Spouse Addition** (Married)
- Applies at all rating levels: **+$104.00** (for 50% example)
- Amount varies slightly by rating: ranges from ~$62 (10%) to ~$209 (100%)

**Child Addition** (Under 18, or 19 if in school, or 23 if in approved school full-time)
- Each child adds: **+$62.00** (for 50% example)
- Amount varies by rating: ranges from ~$39 (10%) to ~$144 (100%)
- Maximum typically 3 children listed
- Additional children may have different rates

**Scenario: Veteran with 1 Spouse + 3 Children**

Spouse Addition             = +$104.00
Child 1 Addition            = +$62.00
Child 2 Addition            = +$62.00
Child 3 Addition            = +$62.00
                            ___________
TOTAL MONTHLY PAYMENT       = $1,365.00
```

**Scenario: Veteran with Spouse Only**
```
Base Veteran (50%)          = $1,075.00

### Important Notes About Dependent Payments

1. **Who Qualifies as a Dependent:**
   - Spouse (married)
   - Children under 18
   - Children 18-23 if enrolled full-time in approved school
   - Stepchildren if legal relationship recognized
   - Children adopted before age 18

2. **When Dependent Payments Stop:**
   - Child turns 18 (unless in school)
   - Child turns 23 (even if in school)
   - Marriage is dissolved (spouse)
   - You remarry (old spouse payment stops, new spouse payment starts)
   - Death of dependent

3. **Dependent Rate Changes:**
   - Rates increase with annual COLA (Cost of Living Adjustment)
   - Changes effective December 1 each year
   - Back pay issued if retroactive

4. **Reporting Changes:**
   - Must report marriage/remarriage
   - Must report children aging off
   - Must report new children born
   - Update through VA.gov or by phone: 1-800-827-1000

## Understanding the Rating Schedule

VA uses a detailed schedule for each condition type. Ratings are based on:

### Key Factors
1. **Severity of Symptoms**: How bad is it?
2. **Functional Limitations**: What can't you do?
3. **Treatment Needed**: What care is required?
4. **Stability**: Is it getting better or worse?

### Example: PTSD Rating Scale

| Rating | Symptoms | Functional Impact |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| 0% | Mild symptoms | No functional impact |
| 10% | Occasional anxiety | Minimal work impact |
| 20% | Regular anxiety/depression | Some work difficulty |
| 30% | Moderate occupational/social impairment | Noticeable limitations |
| 50% | Significant anxiety/depression, panic attacks | Major work limitations |
| 70% | Severe reactions, frequent panic, suicidal thoughts | Cannot work |
| 100% | Complete disability | Cannot function |

## Combined Ratings Explained

### Why Not Just Add Them?
- VA recognizes that multiple disabilities interact
- Total doesn't equal sum of parts
- Combines using special formula

### The Formula
VA multiplies the remainders and subtracts from 100%:

**Example**:
- First condition: 50%
- Second condition: 30%
- Calculation: 50 + (30 × 50% remaining) = 50 + 15 = 65%
- **Result: 65% combined rating**

### Combined Ratings Table
VA maintains official Combined Ratings Table for accuracy:
- Available at https://www.va.gov/disability/combined-ratings/
- Shows all possible combinations
- Ensures consistent results

### When Multiple Ratings Give Different Values
VA always uses the highest benefit formula:
- If two conditions = 65%
- Can't get 65% from table
- VA rounds in veteran's favor

## Effective Dates

### When Do Benefits Start?
**Original Claim**: Earliest of:
- Date VA received claim
- 1 year before VA received claim (if sufficient evidence within year)

**Claim for Increase**: Date of claim (if condition worsened between decision and claim)

**Reopened Claim**: Date application received (not retroactive)

### Back Pay Calculation
- Monthly rate × number of months
- Paid in lump sum for delayed processing
- Direct deposit to your account

## Special Monthly Compensation (SMC) {#smc}

**What Is SMC?** Extra money on top of your percentage rating for severe disabilities.

**When It Applies:** Your condition is so severe that your percentage rating doesn't capture how disabled you are.

**Examples of SMC Situations:**
- Loss of both hands → Can't do job that requires hands → SMC instead of continuing to 70% rating
- Blindness combined with amputation → Extra disability from combined effect → SMC
- Needs someone to help with daily care (bathing, dressing) → Aide & Attendance SMC
- Housebound due to multiple service-connected conditions → Housebound SMC

### SMC Rates vs. Percentage Ratings

**Key Difference**: SMC is NOT a percentage. It's a fixed monthly amount for a specific severe condition.

**Examples (2026 rates)**:

| SMC Type | Reason | Monthly Rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Aide & Attendance (A&A)** | Unable to bathe, dress, eat without help | $2,151 |
| **Housebound** | Cannot leave home unassisted | $2,151 |
| **Bilateral (loss of both)** | Both hands AND both feet OR both eyes + both hands/feet | $7,894 |
| **Loss of One Hand & One Foot** | Bilateral amputation | $4,464 |
| **Loss of Both Hands** | Cannot perform work | $4,464 |
| **Paraplegic** | Loss of use of lower limbs | $3,532 |
| **Blindness + other severe** | Loss of eyesight plus another severe disability | $3,532 to $7,894 |

---

### Real SMC Examples

**Example 1: Loss of Both Legs (Paraplegic)**
```
Normal Rating Path:
- Loss of both legs alone might be 70–80% rating
- Monthly payment at 70%: $1,716

SMC Path (Paraplegic):
- Qualifies for SMC - Paraplegic rating
- Monthly payment: $3,532
- **Difference: $1,816/month extra**

Why SMC Instead of Rating?:
- Paraplegic requires 24/7 care, can't work
- Cannot evacuate in emergency
- Rating percentage doesn't capture total disability
- Fixed SMC amount better reflects reality
```

**Example 2: Loss of Both Hands + Severe PTSD**
```
Option A: Combine Two Ratings:
- Loss of both hands: 70%
- PTSD: 70%
- Combined rating table: ~91%
- Payment at 91%: ~$2,500

Option B: Qualify for Bilateral SMC:
- Loss of both hands PLUS another major disability
- Becomes "Bilateral" SMC condition
- Fixed amount: $7,894/month
- **Difference: $5,394/month higher**

Key Point:
- When multiple severe disabilities exist together
- SMC often pays more than percentage rating would
- VA should be proactively identifying SMC eligibility
```

**Example 3: Aide & Attendance**
```
Scenario: 60% rating but needs caregiver

Note: Don't have 100% rating (only 60%)

Standard 60% Payment:
- 60% rating = $1,362/month
- No extra for needing caregiver

With Aide & Attendance SMC:
- Keep 60% rating ($1,362)
- Add Aide & Attendance over it: +$2,151
- Total: $3,513/month
- **$2,151/month additional for caregiver costs**
```

---

### How to Qualify for SMC

You typically need:
1. **Original rating** (10% or higher) + **one** of:
   - Loss of limb/hand/foot/eye
   - Housebound due to disability
   - Need aide/attendant for daily care
   - Spouse unable to care for you due to combat-related injury

2. **Request it**: SMC doesn't automatically apply; often must request it

3. **Prove the need**: Medical evidence showing the severe limitation

## Individual Unemployable (IU) / TDIU {#tdiu}

**TDIU = "Total Disability Individual Unemployable"** (same as IU — different name)

**What It Is:** 100% payment ($3,738/month) even though your rating might be only 50% or 60%.

**When You Qualify:**
- Have rating of **50%** OR **60%** (or any combination totaling 50+%)
- AND medically unable to work due to service-connected conditions
- AND unable to get substantially gainful employment

---

### TDIU vs. 100% Rating: The Difference

**100% Permanent & Total (P&T)**
- Actual 100% rating (rare for non-SMC)  
- Won't be re-examined
- Lifetime benefits unless you earn too much money
- Automatic if medically justified

**TDIU / Individual Unemployable**
- Rating is 50% or 60% (or combination)
- But rated at 100% payment level
- Subject to periodic re-evaluation to confirm still unemployable
- Must prove you tried to work and couldn't

---

### How to Get TDIU

1. **Check eligibility:**
   - Do you have 50% or higher rating? ✓
   - Medically unable to work? ✓
   - Applied for jobs without success? ✓

2. **Request TDIU:**
   - File using VA Form 21-8940
   - Submit with evidence:
     - Work history (jobs you tried, couldn't do)
     - Medical opinion from doctor: "unemployable due to disabilities"
     - Job rejection letters (if you applied)
     - Vocational assessment

3. **VA Reviews:**
   - VA determines if enough severity for unemployment
   - Usually takes 3–6 months
   - Decision letter explains rating vs. IU rating

---

### Real TDIU Example

```
Scenario: 60% Rating (Back Pain + PTSD)

Standard 60% Payment:
- Monthly: $1,362
- Can work part-time if possible

With TDIU Approval:
- Rating stays 60% (unchanged)
- Payment raised to 100%: $3,738/month
- **Monthly increase: $2,376**
- **Yearly increase: $28,512**

Requirements to Keep TDIU:
- Can't work (even part-time)
- Medical evidence continues
- May be re-examined periodically
- If you're able to work again, TDIU ends
```

---

## Permanent & Total (P&T)

**P&T** means your condition(s) are so severe that:
- You're rated 100%
- VA won't re-examine you
- Benefits continue for life (if you don't earn too much income)

### Who Gets P&T Status?

Typically you need:
- **Actual 100% rating** from VA decision AND
- **Deemed permanent** by VA rater

Not everyone with 100% gets P&T. Some 100% ratings are reviewable. But once rated P&T, they rarely re-examine.

### P&T Benefits Over Time

- **Lifetime benefits** without re-examination
- **Dependents stay on your rating indefinitely** (under certain income rules)
- **Cannot be decreased** unless clear fraud
- **Survivor benefits protected** for family after your death

## Increasing Your Rating {#increase}

## Increasing Your Rating {#increase}

### When to File for an Increase

File a claim for increase if:
- Your condition has **definitely worsened** since your last rating
- **1+ year has passed** since your original rating (to show genuine change)
- **Medical evidence supports it** (doctors note worsening, new test results, etc.)

**Don't file if**: Condition is stable or improving.

---

### What Evidence Helps Your Increase Application

✅ **Recent Medical Records** (Most important)
- Doctor notes showing new symptoms
- Lab results showing decline in function
- Imaging showing progression

✅ **New C&P Exam Results**
- Medical evaluation specifically for increase
- Shows current severity
- Compares to original rating

✅ **Doctor's Statement About Worsening**
- Letter from your doctor explaining deterioration
- Medical basis for why worse than before
- Examples of functional decline

✅ **Work History Documentation**
- Lost job due to disability worsening
- Reduced hours at work
- Can't perform previous duties anymore

✅ **Your Own Statement**
- Describe activities you CAN'T do now (that you could before)
- Specific time period of deterioration
- Impact on daily life

---

### Increase Timeline

1. **File Form 21-526EZ** (same application as original claim)
   - Check "Increase" box
   - Identify condition you want increased
   - Submit supporting evidence

2. **VA Schedules C&P Exam** (2-4 weeks)
   - Medical evaluation for current status
   - Compared to your file

3. **Rating Decision Issued** (2-3 months after exam)
   - New rating percentage (if approved)
   - Effective date = claim date
   - Backpay from claim date to approval date

**Total Timeline: Usually 3–6 months**

---

### Increase Example

```
Original Rating (from years ago):
- Condition: PTSD
- Rating: 20% ($343/month)
- Effective: January 2010

Current Situation (January 2026):
- PTSD got worse (more panic attacks, can't work)
- File for increase
- New C&P exam shows moderate-severe symptoms
- Approved for 40%

Increase Effective Date:
- January 2026 (when you filed for increase)
- NOT back to 2010 (see Effective Dates guide)

New Payment:
- 40% rating = $730/month
- Old payment = $343/month
- Increase = $387/month

Backpay Calculation:
- File: January 2026
- Approval: September 2026 (9 months)
- Increase: $387/month × 9 months = $3,483 backpay
- Back pay as lump sum in September
- Future payments at $730/month
```

## Appealing Your Rating {#appeals}

### Why Ratings Are Appealed

- **Too low**: VA's rating doesn't match your actual condition severity
- **Effective date wrong**: Should be earlier; should have more backpay
- **Evidence not considered**: VA missed important medical records
- **Criteria misapplied**: VA used wrong standard for your condition

---

### Three Appeal Options

**Option 1: Higher-Level Review (HLR)** — Best if VA made a mistake  
- **Timeline**: 4–6 months
- **Cost**: Free
- **What happens**: Senior rater reviews YOUR SAME FILE (no new evidence)
- **Good for**: VA clearly misunderstood existing evidence; obvious error
- **File**: VA Form 20-0996

**Option 2: Supplemental Claim** — Best if you have new evidence  
- **Timeline**: 4–6 months
- **Cost**: Free
- **What happens**: Add new medical records, doctor opinions, evidence VA didn't have
- **Good for**: Got diagnosis after rating; have new doctor opinion; better proof
- **File**: VA Form 20-0995

**Option 3: Board Appeal (VA.gov Decision Review)** — Most formal  
- **Timeline**: 12–24 months+
- **Cost**: Free (unless hire attorney; can cost $5,000–$15,000)
- **What happens**: Case goes to federal hearing judge; you can testify
- **Good for**: Complex case; legal issues; strong evidence; want hearing
- **File**: VA Form 10182 (Notice of Disagreement)

---

### Example: Appealing a Low Rating

```
Original Decision:
- Condition: Back pain
- Rating assigned: 10%
- You disagree: Should be 30-40%

Appeal Strategy (Supplemental Claim):
- Gather better evidence:
  - Recent MRI showing disc herniation (new evidence)
  - Orthopedic surgeon letter: "Unable to perform heavy labor" (new evidence)
  - Work termination letter showing job loss due to disability (new evidence)

Submit New Evidence:
- File Form 20-0995 with all three new pieces
- Explain why new evidence changes case
- Keep copy for your records

VA Reviews With New Evidence:
- Considers original file PLUS new evidence
- Re-rates condition based on combined evidence
- Issues new decision (usually within 4-6 months)

Possible Outcome - Appeal Approved:
- New rating: 40% (instead of 10%)
- Effective: Original claim date
- Difference: $387/month
- Monthly increase going forward: $387
- Backpay since original date: $387/month × 180 months = $69,660 lump sum
```

## Decreasing Your Rating (VA Can Do This)

### When VA Initiates a Decrease

VA can lower your rating if:
- Medical evidence shows **significant improvement**
- Condition is **likely to improve further**
- Usually only for conditions likely to heal (fractures, surgery recovery)

**Note**: VA rarely decreases mental health ratings (PTSD, depression, anxiety) because these are considered unlikely to improve significantly.

---

### If You Get a Decrease Notice

1. **Don't panic** — You have 30 days to respond

2. **Gather evidence**:
   - Medical records showing condition still serious
   - Doctor's statement: "Still disabled despite some improvement"
   - Evidence of functional limitations

3. **File objection** (informal letter)
   - Explain why decrease wrong
   - Submit supporting evidence
   - Mail to VA regional office

4. **Request exam** if needed
   - Ask VA to schedule new C&P exam
   - Get medical documentation of continued disability
   - Can help dispute the decrease

5. **Formal appeal** if decrease issued:
   - File Form 20-0996 (Higher-Level Review)
   - Or Form 20-0995 (Supplemental Claim with new evidence)
   - Explain why rating shouldn't decrease

## Zero Percent (0%) Ratings {#zero}

### What Does 0% Mean?

**0% rating means:**
- Service connection **IS** established (condition IS service-related)
- Condition **IS** confirmed by medical evidence
- But currently **NOT disabling enough** for percentage rating
- Can be increased later if condition worsens

**You still get:**
- Full VA healthcare benefits
- Eligibility for VA home loan
- Can add dependents (they also get 0% rating benefits)
- Can file for increase anytime

**You don't get:**
- Monthly disability payment ($0/month)
- Dependency payments for spouse/children initially

---

### Why You Might Get 0%

- **Condition present but stable** (e.g., old scar with minimal pain)
- **No functional limitation** yet (e.g., condition exists but doesn't prevent work)
- **New diagnosis not yet affecting function** (e.g., early arthritis)
- **VA acknowledges service connection but rates it mild**

---

### Filing for Increase from 0%

Anytime your condition worsens:
1. File Form 21-526EZ (Claim for increase)
2. Submit new medical evidence (doctor note, test results, C&P exam)
3. Show how condition now affects work/life
4. VA rates you - can jump to 10%, 20%, or higher

**No time limit** — Can file for increase any time after 0% rating.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I get paid while my claim is being rated?**  
A: No. You must be approved and effective date assigned first. Then you get lump sum backpay + ongoing monthly payments.

**Q: Can my rating go down?**  
A: Yes, but usually only for conditions likely to improve. VA rarely decreases mental health ratings.

**Q: Will my rating increase every year?**  
A: No. Your rating stays same unless your condition changes or you file for increase.

**Q: Can I work and still get paid my rating?**  
A: Yes! Disability payment is yours regardless of employment. You can work while rated disabled.

**Q: What if I disagree with the C&P exam doctor?**  
A: You can appeal and get second opinion. Submit private medical records/opinions with appeal.

**Q: Do spouse/kids affect my rating?**  
A: No. Rating is based on YOUR condition only. Dependents get added payments (different calculation).

## Key Takeaways

1. **Rating = Percentage → Payment**: Your % determines your monthly money (0% = $0; 100% = $3,738)

2. **Higher rating = More money**: Each percentage increase = hundreds more per month

3. **VA looks at function**: How does condition affect work, daily activities? Not just diagnosis

4. **Evidence quality matters**: Good medical documentation helps get higher rating

5. **Service connection first**: Must establish SC before rating assigned

6. **Combine multiple disabilities**: VA uses formula, not simple addition

7. **SMC possible**: Severe disabilities may get SMC instead of percentage rating

8. **TDIU/IU = 100% pay**: 50-60% rating can get 100% payment if unemployable

9. **P&T = lifetime benefits**: 100% "Permanent & Total" not re-examined

10. **Appeal if too low**: Many approvals are higher on appeal; don't give up

11. **New evidence wins**: Better documentation on appeal often succeeds

12. **Annual increases**: COLA adjustment every December
