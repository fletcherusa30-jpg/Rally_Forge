# VA Dependent Rates Guide (2026)

Complete breakdown of how dependent spouse and children affect your VA disability compensation.

## Understanding Dependent Payments

### What is a Dependent?
VA recognizes the following as dependents:
- **Spouse**: Legal husband or wife
- **Children**: Biological, adopted, or stepchildren
  - Qualify if under 18 years old
  - Qualify if 18-23 and enrolled full-time in approved school
  - Maximum benefit period: age 23 or school completion

### How Much Does Each Dependent Add?

## Important Information About Dependents

### Birth of New Child
- Report to VA immediately
- New child qualifies for dependent rate addition
- Effective date: typically the month of birth
- Back pay issued if delayed

### Marriage
- Report marriage within 30 days
- Spouse dependent addition starts
- Effective date: date of marriage
- Affects also SMC (Special Monthly Compensation) if applicable

### Divorce/Remarriage
- Report within 30 days
- Old spouse rate stops immediately
- New spouse rate begins next month (after divorce final)
- Can result in temporary payment adjustment

### Child Aging Out (Turns 18)
- If child NOT in school: dependent status stops at 18
- If child IN approved school: continues until 23 or graduation
- Report to VA to avoid overpayment
- If you don't report, VA will detect and seek repayment

### Child Aging Out (Turns 23)
- All dependent status ending at 23
- Even if still in school
- Payment automatically reduced by child's rate amount

### Going to School (18-20)
- If child in approved school full-time: still qualifies
- Part-time school: may NOT qualify
- Must submit proof of enrollment
- Annual certification may be required

### Dependent Validation
- VA sends periodic dependent verification letters
- Must respond to confirm dependent status
- Required typically every 2-3 years
- Can be done online at VA.gov

## Dependent Rate Changes

### Annual COLA Increases
- All rates increase with COLA (Cost of Living Adjustment)
- 2024 COLA: 3.2%
- 2025 COLA: 2.5%
- Typically effective December 1
- Apply to both veteran and dependent portions

### Recent Rate Changes (2024-2026)
- 2024: Increased 3.2% due to inflation
- 2025: Increased 2.5% (rates shown are 2026 projections)
- 2026: Rates effective December 1, 2025
- All rates adjust automatically; no claim needed

## Special Situations

### Aid and Attendance/Housebound
- Additional payments for severely disabled veterans
- Does NOT depend on dependents
- Separate from dependent rates
- SM (Aid & Attendance): +$2,151/month (2026)

### SMC (Special Monthly Compensation)
- Additional payment beyond disability rating
- May have dependent additions
- Examples: Loss of limbs, blindness, severe burns
- Dependent rates similar structure to disability rates

### TDIU (Total Disability Individual Unemployable)
- Provides 100% payment at 50% or 60% rating
- Dependent additions same as 100% rating
- More valuable than straight percentage rating
- Requires application

### Retroactive Dependent Changes
- If you report late, usually no back pay
- Exception: Birth (baby born counts from birth month)
- Exception: Marriage (counts from marriage date)
- Report changes within 30 days to avoid issues

## FAQ - Dependent Rates

**Q: Do I have to report getting married?**
A: Yes, VA requires notification within 30 days. Dependent rate begins the month of marriage.

**Q: What if I have a child after my rating decision?**
A: Report the birth immediately. VA adjusts payment to include child dependent rate retroactive to birth month.

**Q: If I have 4+ children, what happens?**
A: First 3 children included in standard dependent calculations. 4th+ children may have individual rates or require separate filing. Contact VA for specifics.

**Q: What about stepchildren?**
A: Stepchildren count if: (1) legally adopted before turning 18, OR (2) parent married before child turned 18 AND child in veteran's household. Document with birth certificate showing parentage.

**Q: Do grandchildren count as dependents?**
A: Generally no, unless legally adopted. Guardianship alone is insufficient.

**Q: When do dependent rates stop?**
A: Spouse: Divorce final. Child: Age 18 (no school), age 23 (in school), or death.

**Q: Does my dependent payment affect my tax situation?**
A: VA disability compensation is non-taxable, including dependent portions. Report $0 income.

**Q: What if VA says I overpaid due to dependent error?**
A: You can request waiver if: (1) Not your fault, AND (2) Paying back would cause hardship. Discuss with VA representative.
