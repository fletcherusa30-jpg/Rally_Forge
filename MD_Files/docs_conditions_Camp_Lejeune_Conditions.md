# Camp Lejeune Water Contamination Presumptive Conditions

**Authority:** 38 CFR §3.309(f) | Updated: 2/12/2026

## Overview
If a veteran, former reservist, or member of the National Guard was exposed to contaminants in the water supply at Camp Lejeune during military service and meets the requirements of §3.307(a)(7), the following diseases shall be service-connected even though there is no record of such disease during service, subject to rebuttable presumption provisions of §3.307(d).

## Presumptive Service-Connected Diseases

### Cancers
1. **Kidney Cancer**
2. **Liver Cancer**
3. **Non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma**
4. **Bladder Cancer**

### Blood & Bone Marrow Disorders
5. **Adult Leukemia**
6. **Multiple Myeloma**
7. **Aplastic Anemia and Other Myelodysplastic Syndromes**

### Neurological Disorders
8. **Parkinson's Disease**

## Qualifying Service Requirements

### Location
**Camp Lejeune, North Carolina** (includes associated satellite installations)

### Time Period
Veterans must have served at Camp Lejeune for at least **30 days** (cumulative, not necessarily consecutive) during:

**August 1, 1953 through December 31, 1987**

### Eligible Personnel
- **Active Duty Military Personnel** (Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard)
- **Reserve Components** (including National Guard)
- **Civilian employees** who worked at Camp Lejeune may be covered under separate provisions

## The Contamination

### Toxic Substances Identified
The water supply at Camp Lejeune was contaminated with:
- **Trichloroethylene (TCE)** - Industrial degreasing solvent
- **Perchloroethylene (PCE)** - Dry cleaning solvent  
- **Benzene** - Fuel/industrial chemical
- **Vinyl Chloride** - Chemical intermediate
- **Other volatile organic compounds (VOCs)**

### Contaminated Water Systems
- **Tarawa Terrace** water treatment plant
- **Hadnot Point** water treatment plant
- Other on-base water distribution systems

### Health Effects
These chemicals are known carcinogens and have been linked to various cancers, blood disorders, and neurological conditions.

## Evidence Requirements

### What Veterans Must Demonstrate:
1. **Service at Camp Lejeune** for at least 30 days between August 1, 1953 and December 31, 1987
2. **Current diagnosis** of one of the eight presumptive conditions
3. **No need to prove** actual water consumption or specific exposure (presumption applies based on presence at the base)

### Documentation Needed:
- **Service records** showing duty station at Camp Lejeune
- **Orders or assignment records** indicating dates stationed at Camp Lejeune
- **Medical evidence** of current diagnosis

### What is NOT Required:
- Direct evidence of drinking contaminated water
- Proof of which specific water system was used
- Evidence linking the specific chemical to the specific disease

## Presumption of Service Connection

Once qualifying service and a diagnosis are established, **VA must presume** service connection unless:

### Exceptions (Presumption Rebutted if:)
1. **Affirmative evidence** the disease was not incurred during service
2. **Affirmative evidence** the disease was caused by a supervening event after service
3. **Affirmative evidence** the disease resulted from the veteran's own willful misconduct

## Related Benefits & Programs

### Camp Lejeune Family Member Program
Family members who lived at Camp Lejeune during the contamination period may be eligible for:
- Reimbursement for out-of-pocket medical expenses related to the 15 covered conditions
- Coverage under the Camp Lejeune Family Member Program (separate from veteran presumptive service connection)

### Healthcare Benefits
Veterans who served at Camp Lejeune may be eligible for:
- **Priority healthcare** enrollment at VA medical facilities
- **No copayments** for treatment of the presumptive conditions
- **Disability compensation** if conditions are service-connected

## Legislative Authority

### Honoring America's Veterans and Caring for Camp Lejeune Families Act of 2012
- Established the 8 presumptive conditions
- Codified at 38 U.S.C. §1112(b)
- Implemented in regulations at 38 CFR §3.309(f)

### PACT Act of 2022
The PACT Act reinforced and may have expanded Camp Lejeune coverage. Check current VA guidance for any updates.

## Special Considerations

### Secondary Service Connection
Other conditions that develop as a result of the presumptive conditions may qualify for secondary service connection under 38 CFR §3.310.

### Surviving Spouses
If a veteran dies from one of the presumptive conditions and was service-connected (or should have been), surviving spouses may be eligible for Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC).

### Effective Dates
Claims for Camp Lejeune presumptive conditions may have retroactive effective dates:
- **General rule:** Date of claim or date entitlement arose, whichever is later
- **Special provisions:** May apply for claims pending when the law was enacted

## How to File a Claim

1. **Gather Evidence:**
   - Service records showing Camp Lejeune duty
   - Medical records with diagnosis
   - VA medical examination (if requested)

2. **Submit VA Form 21-526EZ** (Application for Disability Compensation)

3. **Indicate Camp Lejeune Service:**
   - Specify dates stationed at Camp Lejeune
   - Identify which presumptive condition(s) claimed

4. **Obtain VA Examination** (if VA requests)

5. **Await Decision:**
   - VA should grant service connection for qualifying conditions
   - Appeal if denied

## Common Claim Denials & Solutions

### "Insufficient Evidence of Service at Camp Lejeune"
**Solution:** Submit additional service records, orders, or buddy statements

### "Disease Not on Presumptive List"
**Solution:** May still pursue direct service connection under 38 CFR §3.303-3.304 with medical nexus evidence

### "Did Not Serve 30 Days"
**Solution:** Verify all periods of service; temporary duty (TDY) and training periods may count
