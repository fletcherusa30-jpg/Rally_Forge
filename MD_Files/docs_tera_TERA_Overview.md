# TERA Overview: Toxic Exposure Risk Activities

**Reference:** VA Toxic Exposure Risk Activities Memorandum  
**Authority:** Part of PACT Act implementation framework  
**Purpose:** Classification system for identifying and evaluating toxic exposure claims

---

## What is TERA?

**TERA (Toxic Exposure Risk Activities)** is VA's comprehensive framework for:
1. **Identifying** potential toxic exposures during military service
2. **Categorizing** exposure types and risk levels
3. **Evaluating** claims based on documented exposures
4. **Screening** veterans for toxic exposure-related conditions
5. **Prioritizing** research and healthcare for exposed populations

---

## TERA Classification System

### Primary Exposure Categories

#### 1. **Airborne Hazards**
- **Burn Pits** (open-air waste incineration)
- **Fine Particulate Matter (PM2.5, PM10)**
- **Sand and dust storms**
- **Industrial air pollution**
- **Smoke from oil well fires**
- **Demolition/construction dust**

**High-Risk Locations:**
- Iraq, Afghanistan, Kuwait (post-1990)
- Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Qatar, UAE, Oman
- Syria, Djibouti, Yemen, Jordan

#### 2. **Chemical Exposures**
- **Herbicide Agents** (Agent Orange, Agent Blue, Agent White, etc.)
- **Industrial chemicals** (solvents, degreasers)
- **Pesticides**
- **Chemical warfare agents** (nerve agents, blister agents)
- **Contaminated water** (PFAS, VOCs, heavy metals)

**High-Risk Locations:**
- Vietnam, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia (1962-1975)
- Korean DMZ (1968-1971)
- Camp Lejeune, NC (1953-1987)
- Other contaminated military installations

#### 3. **Radiation Exposure**
- **Ionizing radiation** from nuclear tests
- **Cleanup operations** at nuclear incident sites
- **Occupational exposure** at nuclear facilities
- **X-ray and medical radiation** (historical)

**High-Risk Activities:**
- Atmospheric nuclear tests (1945-1962)
- Hiroshima/Nagasaki occupation (1945-1946)
- Nuclear cleanup sites (Enewetak, Palomares, Thule)
- Gaseous diffusion plants (Paducah, Portsmouth, Oak Ridge)

#### 4. **Infectious/Biological Hazards**
- **Endemic diseases** in theater of operations
- **Vector-borne illnesses** (malaria, leishmaniasis)
- **Waterborne pathogens**
- **Biological warfare agent exposure**

**High-Risk Locations:**
- Southwest Asia (Gulf War infectious diseases)
- Afghanistan (leishmaniasis, Q fever)
- Tropical/subtropical deployment locations

#### 5. **Occupational/Environmental Hazards**
- **Asbestos** (ships, buildings, vehicles)
- **Lead** (paint, ammunition, batteries)
- **Noise** (aircraft, artillery, vehicles)
- **Extreme temperatures** (heat/cold injury)
- **Fuel/petroleum products**

## TERA Screening Process

### VA Toxic Exposure Screening (TES)

All veterans enrolled in VA healthcare receive periodic TERA screening:

#### screening Questions:
1. **Deployment History**
   - Locations served
   - Dates of service
   - Military occupational specialty (MOS)

2. **Environmental Exposures**
   - Burn pit proximity
   - Chemical/herbicide contact
   - Radiation activities
   - Water contamination

3. **Occupational Exposures**
   - MOS-specific hazards
   - Equipment maintenance
   - Industrial work

4. **Current Health Concerns**
   - Respiratory symptoms
   - Neurological issues
   - Cancer diagnoses
   - Chronic unexplained illnesses

#### Screening Outcomes:
- **Positive Screen:** Flagged for further evaluation
- **Documented Exposure:** Entered in VA medical record
- **Referral:** Specialty care if indicated
- **Claims Assistance:** VSO referral for benefits claims

## TERA Risk Stratification

### High-Risk Activities (Presumptive Service Connection)

**Tier 1: Presumptive Locations/Exposures**
- Service in presumptive location = automatic exposure presumption
- No need to prove individual exposure
- Codified in 38 CFR Part 3

**Examples:**
- Vietnam service → Agent Orange exposed (38 CFR §3.309(e))
- Iraq 2003-2011 → Burn pit exposed (38 CFR §3.320)
- Camp Lejeune 1953-1987 → Water contamination exposed (38 CFR §3.309(f))

### Moderate-Risk Activities (Evidence-Based Connection)

**Tier 2: Documented But Not Presumptive**
- Deployment to location with known hazards but no regulatory presumption
- Requires medical nexus opinion linking exposure to condition
- May use TERA documentation to support claim

**Examples:**
- Philippines (Agent Orange testing sites)
- Germany (Cold War chemical exposures)
- Panama (herbicide use)

### Lower-Risk Activities (General Service)

**Tier 3: Occupational/Incidental Exposure**
- Common military occupational hazards
- Requires direct evidence of exposure
- Medical nexus required

**Examples:**
- Aircraft maintenance (fuel, solvents)
- Vehicle mechanics (asbestos, lead)
- Shipboard service (asbestos, radiation)

## TERA Documentation & Claims

### How TERA Supports VA Claims

#### 1. **Exposure Documentation**
- **Service records** automatically reviewed for TERA indicators
- **Deployment locations** cross-referenced with exposure databases
- **MOS codes** linked to occupational hazards

#### 2. **Presumption Determination**
- **Automated screening** identifies presumptive exposures
- **Regulatory compliance** with 38 CFR Part 3
- **Claim routing** to appropriate decision pathways

#### 3. **Medical Nexus Development**
- **TERA data** provided to C&P examiners
- **Exposure history** incorporated in medical opinions
- **Presumptive pathways** clearly identified

### Evidence Hierarchy

**Strongest Evidence:**
1. **Presumptive exposure** (38 CFR codified)
2. **Service records** showing deployment to hazard location
3. **Unit records** documenting exposures
4. **Individual dosimetry** or monitoring data

**Supporting Evidence:**
5. **MOS-based exposure** (occupational)
6. **Buddy statements** corroborating exposures
7. **Declassified documents** on unit activities
8. **Post-service TERA screening** results

## TERA & PACT Act Integration

### How PACT Act Uses TERA Framework

1. **Exposure Identification**
   - PACT Act presumptions based on TERA exposure categories
   - New regulations (§3.320 series) follow TERA classification

2. **Claims Processing**
   - TERA screening triggers PACT Act review
   - Automated identification of PACT-eligible veterans

3. **Research & Policy**
   - TERA data informs future presumption decisions
   - Exposure patterns guide VA research priorities

### PACT Act Presumptive Locations = TERA High-Risk Zones

**Airborne Hazards (Burn Pits):**
- Southwest Asia theater (Iraq, Kuwait, etc.)
- Afghanistan, Syria, Djibouti, Uzbekistan
- All TERA locations with documented burn pit operations

**Chemical Hazards (Herbicides):**
- Vietnam, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia
- Korean DMZ, Guam, Johnston Atoll
- All TERA locations with documented herbicide use

**Radiation Hazards:**
- Nuclear test sites
- Cleanup operations
- All TERA radiation-risk activities per §3.309(d)

## TERA Resources & Tools

### For Veterans

#### VA Toxic Exposure Screening Tool
- **Online:** VA.gov (Patient Portal)
- **In-Person:** VA primary care visits
- **Purpose:** Document exposures for medical record

#### Exposure Tracking
- **Airborne Hazards and Open Burn Pit Registry**
  - Self-reported deployment exposures
  - Health assessment questionnaire
  - Links to VA benefits information

- **Agent Orange Registry**
  - Health exam for herbicide-exposed veterans
  - Documents exposure and current health

- **Ionizing Radiation Registry**
  - Exam for radiation-exposed veterans

### For Claims Representatives

#### TERA Claims Development
1. **Identify exposure** using service records
2. **Classify exposure** per TERA categories
3. **Determine presumption** eligibility
4. **Request C&P exam** with exposure context
5. **Link diagnosis** to exposure pathway

#### Key Databases
- **Defense Manpower Data Center (DMDC):** Deployment records
- **National Archives:** Unit records, test site documentation
- **VA Exposure databases:** Location/MOS cross-reference
- **PACT Act regulations:** 38 CFR §3.309, §3.320 series

## Practical Application

### Example: Post-9/11 Veteran with Respiratory Issues

**TERA Screening Reveals:**
- Deployment: Iraq 2007-2008
- Location: Camp Victory (documented burn pit)
- MOS: 88M (Motor Transport Operator)
- Current: Chronic sinusitis, asthma

**TERA Classification:**
- **Exposure Category:** Airborne Hazards (burn pit)
- **Risk Level:** High (presumptive location)
- **Regulatory Pathway:** 38 CFR §3.320 (PACT Act)

**Claims Outcome:**
- **Presumptive Service Connection** granted
- **No medical nexus required** (presumption applies)
- **Conditions:** Asthma, sinusitis service-connected
