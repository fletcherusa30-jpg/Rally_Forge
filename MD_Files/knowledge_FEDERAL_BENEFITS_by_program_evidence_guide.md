# Evidence for VA Claims

How to submit evidence that supports your disability claim.

## Types of Evidence

### Medical Evidence (Strongest)
- Current diagnosis from doctor or VA
- Records showing diagnosis, treatment, symptoms
- Lab results or imaging
- Prescription medications
- Frequency and ongoing treatment

**Example**: VA records show PTSD diagnosis, monthly therapy, prescribed sertraline

### Nexus/Opinion Evidence
- Doctor's letter connecting condition to service
- Explains HOW service caused condition
- Can be from private doctor or VA
- Strong when specific to your service

**Example**: "Veteran's PTSD directly related to combat exposure during Iraq deployment, 2005-2007"

### Lay Evidence (Buddy Statements)
- Statement from fellow veteran
- Describes what they witnessed
- Your condition as they saw it
- Personal knowledge of your service

**Example**: "I served with Vet in Afghanistan. Witnessed IED explosion where he was injured. Can confirm combat trauma and current symptoms."

### Service Records
- DD-214 discharge papers
- Military medical records
- Duty assignment records
- Official service verification

**Example**: DD-214 confirms combat zone deployment; medical records show treatment during service

### Personal Records
- Medical bills showing treatment
- Medication bottles with dates
- Appointment cards
- Insurance claims

## How to Submit Evidence

### Before Filing Claim
1. Gather all medical records from doctors/hospitals
2. Get statements from family about symptoms
3. Obtain buddies' statements
4. Collect old medical documentation

### With Initial Claim
- Upload all documents to VA.gov
- Clearly label what each document shows
- Include cover sheet listing all documents
- Keep copies for your records

### After Claim is Filed
- Submit new evidence as "Supplemental Claim"
- VA Form 20-0995
- Can include newly obtained records
- More evidence can improve rating

## Writing Lay Evidence

### Components
1. **Identity**: Your name, service dates, condition
2. **Relationship**: How you know the veteran
3. **Observations**: What you personally witnessed
4. **Timing**: When you observed symptoms
5. **Consistency**: Whether symptoms are ongoing
6. **Impact**: How it affects daily life

### Lay Statement Template
```
I, [Name], am writing to support [Veteran Name]'s
VA disability claim for [Condition].

I [relationship - served with, family member, friend]
from [dates] to [dates].

I observed [specific symptoms/behaviors] during
[time period]. [Give specific examples].

These symptoms [continue/have improved] as of [date].

This condition affects [describe impact on daily life].

I believe this condition is related to [service event/
experience].

Sincerely,
[Signature]
```

## Getting Medical Evidence

### From VA
- Request medical records from VA Regional Office
- Provide claim number and condition
- Usually free, takes 2-4 weeks

### From Private Doctor
- Write letter requesting opinion on:
  - Current diagnosis
  - Service connection (if possible)
  - Course of treatment
  - Prognosis
- Request by specific date (give 2-3 week notice)
- May require office visit

### Obtaining Records from Past
- Private medical: Contact clinic/hospital directly
- Military medical: NPRC (National Personnel Records Center)
- VA records: VA.gov or local VA hospital

## Common Evidence Mistakes

### ❌ Avoid These

1. **Submitting only symptoms list**
   - ✓ Instead: Include medical records confirming diagnosis

2. **Getting letter without service connection explanation**
   - ✓ Instead: Ask doctor specifically to explain nexus

3. **Buddy statement with no details**
   - ✓ Instead: Specific examples of witnessed symptoms/events

4. **Old evidence without current status**
   - ✓ Instead: Include recent medical visit/exam

5. **Submitting evidence VA already has**
   - ✓ Instead: Check VA records first, add new evidence

6. **No explanation of how evidence is relevant**
   - ✓ Instead: Include cover letter explaining connection


## Evidence Ratings

### Highly Probative
- Medical diagnosis from VA hospital
- Doctor's nexus letter
- Multiple doctors confirming diagnosis
- Long-term treatment records

### Moderately Probative
- Private doctor's diagnosis (without nexus)
- Contemporaneous medical records
- Lay statements from credible witnesses
- Evidence of treatment initiation around service

### Less Probative
- Self-reported symptoms only
- Statements without specifics
- Evidence from unclear time period
- Inconsistent documentation

## Special Evidence Situations

### For PTSD
- Stressor statements (what caused the PTSD)
- Corroboration of stressor
- Mental health treatment records
- C-file review (official military records)

### For Presumptive Conditions
- Service in qualifying location/time
- Agent Orange exposure (Vietnam)
- Burn pits (Iraq/Afghanistan)
- Gulf War service
- Camp Lejeune residence

### For Secondary Conditions
- Connection to primary condition
- Medical causation explanation
- Treatment specifically for secondary condition
