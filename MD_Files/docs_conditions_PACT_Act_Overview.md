# PACT Act Overview

## Sergeant First Class Heath Robinson Honoring our Promise to Address Comprehensive Toxics (PACT) Act of 2022

### Legislative Authority
- **Enacted:** August 10, 2022
- **Public Law:** 117-168
- **Presidential Signature:** President Joseph R. Biden Jr.
- **Purpose:** Expand VA health care and benefits for veterans exposed to burn pits, Agent Orange, and other toxic substances

---

## What is the PACT Act?

The PACT Act is the **most significant expansion of VA health care and benefits in decades**. It:

1. **Expands presumptive service connection** for conditions related to toxic exposures
2. **Adds new presumptive locations** for Agent Orange and other herbicide exposures
3. **Creates new presumptions** for burn pit and airborne hazard exposures
4. **Extends coverage periods** and eliminates time limits for many conditions
5. **Expands VA health care eligibility** for toxic-exposed veterans
6. **Improves the claims process** for environmental exposure claims

---

## Key Expansions by Category

### 1. Burn Pit & Airborne Hazard Exposures

#### New Presumptive Locations (38 CFR §3.320 series)
**Post-9/11 Service:**
- Afghanistan, Syria, Djibouti, Uzbekistan (on or after 9/19/2001)
- Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon, Yemen (on or after 9/11/2001)
- **Southwest Asia theater** (Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, etc.)
- Somalia (on or after 8/2/1990)

#### New Presumptive Conditions
**Chronic Respiratory Diseases (38 CFR §3.320):**
- Asthma
- Rhinitis
- Sinusitis (including rhinosinusitis)
- **No time limit** for manifestation
- **Non-compensable ratings qualify**

**Rare Respiratory Cancers (38 CFR §3.320):**
- Squamous cell carcinoma of larynx/trachea
- Adenocarcinoma of trachea
- Lung cancers (adenosquamous, large cell, sarcomatoid, carcinoid, salivary gland-type)

**Genitourinary Cancers (38 CFR §3.320a):**
- Bladder cancer (including overlapping sites)
- Ureter cancer (including ureteric orifice, urachus)

**Blood & Bone Marrow Cancers (38 CFR §3.320b):**
- Acute leukemias
- Chronic leukemias  
- Multiple myelomas (including MGUS)
- Myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS)
- Myelofibrosis

### 2. Agent Orange & Herbicide Exposures

#### New Presumptive Locations
**Thailand Military Bases (1961-1975):**
- U-Tapao Royal Thai Navy Airfield
- Ubon Royal Thai Air Force Base
- Nakhon Phanom Royal Thai Air Force Base
- Udorn Royal Thai Air Force Base
- Takhli Royal Thai Air Force Base
- Korat Royal Thai Air Force Base
- Don Muang International Airport (any air base in vicinity)

**Laos (1965-1975):**
- Military assignments in Laos

**Cambodia (at Mimot or Krek, Kampong Cham Province):**
- Service on or after 4/16/1969 through 4/30/1969

**Guam or American Samoa:**
- Service between 1/9/1962 - 7/31/1980 (Guam)
- Service 1/9/1962 - 7/31/1980 aboard ship that had docked at Guam or American Samoa

**Johnston Atoll:**
- Service on or after 1/1/1972 - 9/30/1977

**Korean DMZ (1968-1971):**
- Service in or near the demilitarized zone

**C-123 Aircraft:**
- Flight crew or maintenance personnel who had regular contact with C-123 aircraft

#### No New Conditions Added (Existing List Under 38 CFR §3.309(e) Applies)
Same conditions as before, but now apply to expanded locations

### 3. Radiation Exposures

#### New Cleanup Sites Added (38 CFR §3.309(d)):
**Enewetak Atoll:**
- Cleanup participation 1/1/1977 - 12/31/1980

**Palomares, Spain:**
- B-52 crash response 1/17/1966 - 3/31/1967

**Thule, Greenland:**
- B-52 crash response 1/21/1968 - 9/25/1968

**Gaseous Diffusion Plants:**
- Paducah, KY; Portsmouth, OH; K25 Oak Ridge, TN
- 250+ days before 2/1/1992

**Amchitka Island, Alaska:**
- Nuclear test participation before 1/1/1974

### 4. Camp Lejeune Water Contamination

**No changes to conditions** - PACT Act reinforced existing presumptions under 38 CFR §3.309(f):

8 Presumptive Conditions:
1. Kidney cancer
2. Liver cancer
3. Non-Hodgkin's lymphoma
4. Adult leukemia
5. Multiple myeloma
6. Parkinson's disease
7. Aplastic anemia/myelodysplastic syndromes
8. Bladder cancer

**Service Requirement:** 30 days at Camp Lejeune between 8/1/1953 - 12/31/1987

## Health Care Eligibility Expansions

### Toxic Exposure Screening
- **All veterans** enrolled in VA health care receive toxic exposure screening
- Identifies potential exposures during military service
- Links to appropriate follow-up care and benefits

### Post-9/11 Veterans
**Enhanced eligibility** for veterans who served:
- In combat theater after 9/11/2001
- Cost-free health care for conditions potentially related to toxic exposure
- Extended enrollment period

### VA Toxic Exposure Screening (TES)
- New standardized screening tool
- Administered during VA primary care visits
- Documents exposures for future claims

---

## Claims Process Improvements

### Faster Processing
- **Presumptions eliminate** need to prove causation
- **Standardized review** for PACT Act conditions
- **Automatic consideration** of service in presumptive locations

### Retroactive Benefits
- **Survivors** of veterans who died from PACT Act conditions may be eligible for retroactive DIC
- **Pending claims** re-evaluated under new presumptions
- **Denied claims** may be reopened as new evidence

### Cost of War Toxic Exposures Fund
- Dedicated funding to cover PACT Act implementation
- Ensures resources for:
  - Claims processing
  - Medical care
  - Research on toxic exposures
  - Outreach to veterans

## Timeline & Implementation

### August 10, 2022: PACT Act Signed
- Immediate eligibility for VA health care enrollment for post-9/11 veterans

### January 1, 2023: First Wave of Presumptions
- Burn pit and toxic exposure presumptions take effect
- Claims can be filed for new presumptive conditions

### Ongoing Implementation
- VA continues to implement regulations
- New CFR sections created (§3.320, §3.320a, §3.320b)
- Updated as of 2/12/2026: Regulations fully in effect

## Who Should File Under the PACT Act?

### You May Be Eligible If:
1. **Post-9/11 Combat Veterans** who served in:
   - Iraq, Afghanistan, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia
   - Syria, Yemen, Djibouti, Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon, Uzbekistan
   - Any Southwest Asia location

2. **Vietnam-Era Veterans** who served in:
   - Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, Guam, American Samoa, Johnston Atoll
   - Korean DMZ (1968-1971)
   - On C-123 aircraft

3. **Radiation-Exposed Veterans** who participated in:
   - Nuclear test cleanups (Enewetak, Palomares, Thule)
   - Gaseous diffusion plant work
   - Amchitka Island nuclear tests

4. **Camp Lejeune Veterans** (1953-1987)

5. **Any veteran** with diagnosis of a PACT Act presumptive condition

## How to File a PACT Act Claim

### Step 1: Gather Evidence
- **Service records** showing duty location
- **Deployment records** for post-9/11 service
- **Medical records** with diagnosis
- **VA medical examinations** (if applicable)

### Step 2: Submit Claim
- **VA Form 21-526EZ** (Disability Compensation)
- **Online:** VA.gov eBenefits portal
- **Mail:** Regional Office
- **In-person:** VA Regional Office or VSO

### Step 3: Indicate PACT Act Eligibility
- Specify service in presumptive location
- Identify PACT Act presumptive condition(s)
- Reference relevant CFR section (e.g., 38 CFR §3.320)

### Step 4: Obtain VA Examination
- VA will schedule Compensation & Pension (C&P) exam if needed
- Examiner confirms diagnosis
- VA determines service connection

### Step 5: Receive Decision
- VA reviews claim under PACT Act presumptions
- Service connection granted if criteria met
- Appeal if denied

## Special Considerations

### Secondary Service Connection
Conditions caused by PACT Act presumptive conditions may also be service-connected under 38 CFR §3.310

### Dependency & Indemnity Compensation (DIC)
Surviving spouses of veterans who died from PACT Act conditions may be eligible for DIC benefits

### Combined Ratings
PACT Act conditions combined with other service-connected disabilities for total rating calculation

### State Benefits
Many states offer additional benefits for service-connected veterans - check state-specific programs
