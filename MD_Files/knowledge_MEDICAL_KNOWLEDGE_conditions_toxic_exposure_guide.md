# Toxic Exposure and Presumptive Conditions

Guide to claim disabilities from toxic exposure including burn pits and Agent Orange.

## PACT Act Exposures (2022 Law)

### What Is the PACT Act?
Expanding and Improving Benefits for Camp Lejeune Family Members and Extending the Department of Veterans Affairs Health Care Benefits
- Presumptive conditions for burn pit/open burn exposure
- Also covers Agent Orange and Camp Lejeune
- Priority processing tier for claims

### Service Locations Covered
- **Iraq**: 2003-present
- **Afghanistan**: 2001-present
- **Burn Pits**: Military waste disposal areas
- **Open Burn/Detonation**: Ammunition and explosives

### Presumptive Conditions (11 + more)
1. Asthma
2. Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
3. Lung cancer
4. Gastrointestinal cancer
5. Glioblastoma
6. Head/neck cancer
7. Kidney cancer
8. Pancreatic cancer
9. Prostate cancer
10. Respiratory conditions
11. And others being added

## Agent Orange Exposure (Vietnam)

## How to File for Presumptive

### Step 1: Check Eligibility
- Service dates match
- Service location matches OR
- Condition on presumptive list

### Step 2: Gather Evidence
- DD-214 or service records
- Medical diagnosis
- Medical treatment records
- Proof of service location (if not obvious from military records)

### Step 3: File Claim
- VA Form 21-526EZ
- Mark as "Presumptive Condition"
- Submit service records
- Submit medical diagnosis
- Include medical records

### Step 4: VA Decides
- If service + condition match presumptive list = automatic approval
- No need for nexus letter
- No need to prove exposure
- VA presumes connection

## Advantages of Presumptive

- **No Nexus Required**: Don't need doctor opinion
- **Easier Approval**: Just prove service + diagnosis
- **Faster Processing**: Priority tier
- **Automatic**: If criteria met, automatic approval
- **Broader**: Can include conditions not usually service-connected

## Evidence Needed for Presumptive

### Minimal Evidence Required
1. **Service Records**: DD-214 proving service location/dates
2. **Medical Diagnosis**: Current diagnosis of presumptive condition
3. **Nothing Else Typically Needed**: VA presumes connection

### Helpful Evidence
- Medical treatment records
- Recent medical exam
- Current medications
- Medical provider statement

## Non-Presumptive Toxic Exposure Claims

### If Condition Not on Presumptive List
- Still can claim exposure-related condition
- Must prove service connection (nexus)
- Requires doctor opinion linking condition to exposure
- Stronger medical evidence needed

### Required Evidence
- Service records proving exposure
- Medical diagnosis
- Nexus letter from doctor explaining:
  - You had toxic exposure during service
  - Your condition resulted from that exposure
  - Medical basis for opinion

## Radiation Example: Non-Presumptive

If you had radiation exposure but condition not on presumptive list:
1. File claim explaining exposure
2. Get medical diagnosis
3. Request doctor's nexus opinion
4. Include detailed exposure information
5. Submit all evidence

## Getting Medical Diagnosis

### From VA
- Schedule VA medical appointment
- Describe symptoms and exposure
- Ask for diagnosis
- Request records for claim

### From Private Doctor
- Schedule appointment
- Describe toxic exposure and symptoms
- Ask for diagnosis
- Request written diagnosis
- Can request nexus letter if helpful

## Priority Processing

PACT Act presumptive claims processed before other claims:
- Faster decision
- Usually within 60 days
- Takes priority at VA Regional Office

## Combined With Other Conditions

If you have both:
- Service-connected condition (existing rating)
- PACT Act/presumptive condition (new claim)

VA combines the ratings using Combined Ratings Table
